4 phases

Move to the next phase only when you meet its "Ready when" line. Being late is fine; getting injured is not.

WEEKS 1–8

1 · Walk–run: build the habit

Three sessions a week, never two days in a row. Each one is a brisk 5-minute walk, then jog/walk intervals. The jogs get longer each week.

WEEKS 9–16

2 · Aerobic base: 5 km

All runs are easy (you can talk in full sentences). Add a 4th short run only if your legs feel fresh. The long run grows from 35 to 50 minutes. Weeks 12 and 16 are easier weeks.

WEEKS 17–24

3 · Build to 10 km

The long run goes up by about 1 km a week: 6 → 7 → 8 → 6 (easier week) → 9 → 10 → 8 → 11 km. From week 17, add one quality session a week: strides first, then tempo, then intervals.

Ready when: you have run 10–11 km in one go at an easy pace, and recovered within 2 days.

WEEKS 25–26

4 · Taper and race

Run about a third less, keep a little speed, sleep well. Race at a pace you could hold for the whole distance, and run the last 2 km faster if you feel strong.

Done when: you have crossed a 10 km finish line. Then choose your next goal: a faster 10 km, or a half marathon.

Easy pace: the most important idea

About 80% of your running should feel easy. Easy running builds your heart, lungs and legs with the least strain on your joints. That matters most for beginners. You don't need a heart-rate monitor: use the talk test.

Easy (Zone 2)You can talk in full sentences. Effort 3–4 out of 10. Most runs.
TempoOnly a few words at a time. Effort 6–7 out of 10. From week 19.
Hard intervalsYou can't talk. Effort 8–9 out of 10. From week 21, short bursts only.
"Effort out of 10" is called RPE (rate of perceived exertion): how hard it feels to you, from 1 (resting) to 10 (all-out). If you can't talk on an easy run, slow down or walk. Walking is part of the plan, not failure.

Safety rules

Do

  • Run on even, softer ground where you can (a track, a park path).
  • Take short, quick steps, landing under your hips.
  • Rest a day between runs in phase 1.
  • Do strength work twice a week: hips, thighs, calves, core.
  • Sleep: the bedtime goal on your dashboard is also your recovery plan.

Don't

  • Add more than one change a week (longer and faster).
  • Run through sharp pain, pain that makes you limp, or pain that is worse the next morning.
  • Make up missed runs by doubling the next one.
  • Wear old or worn-out shoes.
Stop and see a doctor if you get chest pain, dizziness or unusual breathlessness. If you have a heart, blood pressure or other health condition, check with a doctor before you start. Pain rule: mild soreness (up to 3 out of 10) that settles during the run is OK. Anything worse, or worse the next day: rest 2 days, then repeat the last easy week.

Week 1 checklist

This week's actions. Ticks are saved on this device.

  • Check your running shoes. Shoes that are old, worn or not made for running → get a pair fitted at a running shop.
  • Baseline: walk briskly for 30 minutes and note how it felt (effort out of 10), plus your weight that morning.
  • Choose 3 fixed days (for example Tue, Thu, Sat) and a safe, flat route.
  • Run 1: 5 min walk + 8 × (jog 1 min, walk 2 min). Easy pace: talk test.
  • Run 2: same as run 1, at least one rest day later.
  • Run 3: same again. If it felt easy and nothing hurts, move to week 2 next week.
  • Score each run in your journal: minutes jogged and effort out of 10.
