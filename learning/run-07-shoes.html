👟 Shoes, surfaces and impact

Track: RunningBeginnerPractice: One shop visit, then a check each month

1 · The core concept

Shoes don't run for you, but a shoe that fits well and still has its cushioning makes running more comfortable. Surfaces matter less than people think: your legs adjust to what's under them. What matters is choosing even ground while you build up, and changing one thing at a time.

📏Fit first
A thumb's width of space at the toes, a snug heel, no pressure points.
🧽Cushioning wears out
The midsole foam gets flat and creased with use.
🛣️Even ground
A track, a park path or a smooth road is easiest while you build up.

3 · Before you start

  • Your current running shoes, to check for wear.
  • If you buy new ones: go in the evening (feet are slightly bigger) and bring your running socks.

4 · Step-by-step

1

Know the parts

▶ Show
  • Outsole: the rubber that grips.
  • Midsole: the foam that cushions. This is the part that wears out.
  • Upper: the fabric that holds your foot.
2

Check the fit

▶ Show
  • About a thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the shoe.
  • Heel snug, no slipping. Wide enough that your little toe isn't squeezed.
  • Choose what feels comfortable when you jog in the shop. Comfort is a good guide.
3

Check for wear

▶ Show
  • Look at the midsole from the side: deep creases and flattened foam mean it's worn.
  • As a rough guide, many shoes last about 500–800 km. Note the date you start a new pair.
  • Your legs aching more than usual on easy runs can also be a sign.
4

Choose your surface

▶ Show
  • Track: even and measured. Good for strides and intervals later.
  • Park path: even and a bit softer. Great for easy runs.
  • Road: harder but even. Fine in good shoes.
  • Uneven trail: watch your ankles. Leave it for later.

5 · Common mistakes and fixes

✗ Shoes that are too smallToes hit the front, especially downhill.
✓ FixGet the thumb's-width gap. Running shoes are often half a size bigger than your everyday shoes.
✗ Running in worn-out shoesThe foam is flat but the upper looks fine.
✓ FixCheck the midsole, not the upper. Write the start date inside the tongue.
✗ Changing shoes and distance in the same weekTwo new loads at once.
✓ FixBreak new shoes in on short easy runs for a week.
✗ Uneven or sloping ground early onTwisted ankles and sore hips.
✓ FixEven surfaces for now. Add variety once you're steady.

6 · Practice drill: this week

  • Check your shoes: midsole creases, outsole worn through, the thumb's-width gap.
  • If they're worn: get a pair fitted at a running shop.
  • Write the start date inside the shoe's tongue.
  • Pick your main easy-run route on even ground.
