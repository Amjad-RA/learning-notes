🏃 Running form basics

Track: RunningBeginnerPractice: 5 min each run, weeks 1–4

1 · The core concept

Good form spreads the work of running across your whole body, so no single joint takes all the impact. The biggest win is where your foot lands: under your hips, not far in front.

🧍Tall posture
Your head, shoulders and hips stay stacked, with a slight lean from the ankles.
💪Relaxed arms
Elbows at about 90°, swinging forward and back, never across your body.
👣Quick, short steps
Your foot lands under your hips with a soft, bent knee.

3 · Before you start

  • Running shoes in good condition (see the Week 1 checklist).
  • A flat, even route for your walk–run sessions.
  • Optional: ask someone to film 10 seconds of you jogging from the side, to compare with the 3D runner.

4 · Step-by-step

1

Stand tall

▶ Show
  • Imagine a string pulling the top of your head up.
  • Lean forward slightly from the ankles, not from the waist.
  • Eyes look 10–20 m ahead, not at your feet.
2

Relax your arms

▶ Show
  • Elbows at about 90°, hands loose, as if holding a crisp without breaking it.
  • Swing them forward and back. Your hands pass your hips and don't cross the middle of your chest.
  • Keep your shoulders low and loose. Shake them out every few minutes.
3

Land under your hips

▶ Show
  • Your foot lands close to under your body, with your knee slightly bent.
  • Landing on the midfoot or the whole foot is fine. Don't force a toe landing.
  • Land quietly. A loud slap means you are reaching too far forward.
4

Short, quick steps

▶ Show
  • To go faster, take quicker steps before you take longer ones.
  • Count your steps for 30 seconds and double it: that's your cadence (steps per minute). Write it down. Tutorial 9 builds on it.

5 · Common mistakes and fixes

✗ OverstridingThe foot lands far ahead of you with a straight knee. It brakes every step and jars the knee.
✓ FixShorter, quicker steps, with the foot landing under your hips. See ✗ Wrong in phase 3.
✗ SlouchingHead down and back rounded, usually when you're tired.
✓ FixEvery 5 minutes: look up, stand tall, relax your shoulders.
✗ Arms crossing your bodyThis twists your upper body and wastes energy.
✓ FixSwing your elbows straight back, as if elbowing someone behind you.
✗ Changing everything at onceThat leads to new aches.
✓ FixWork on one point per run, in the order of the steps above.

6 · Practice drill: this week

  • Run 1: during each jog, check posture only (step 1).
  • Run 2: arms only (step 2).
  • Run 3: quiet landing under your hips (step 3).
  • Count your cadence once (30 s × 2) and write it in your journal.
