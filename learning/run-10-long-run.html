🛣️ The long run

Track: RunningIntermediatePractice: Once a week, weeks 9–24

1 · The core concept

The weekly long run builds the endurance you need for 10 km. It is run slowly, at talk-test pace, and it grows by about 1 km a week, with an easier week every 4th week. By week 24 you will have run 11 km, so race day holds no surprises.

🐢Slow on purpose
Easy pace is what makes long runs safe and repeatable.
🪜+1 km a week
6 → 7 → 8 → 6 → 9 → 10 → 8 → 11 km in weeks 17–24.
💧Practise for race day
Water, food before, and shoes: try them on long runs.

3 · Before you start

  • You can run 5 km comfortably (end of phase 2).
  • A route you can extend, or a loop you can repeat.
  • A small water bottle for runs over about an hour, especially in heat.

4 · Step-by-step

1

Build: about +1 km a week

▶ Show
  • Weeks 17–19: 6, 7, 8 km.
  • Same easy effort as your other runs. Walking 1 minute is fine.
2

Easier week: shorter

▶ Show
  • Week 20: back to 6 km. Your body catches up.
3

Build again

▶ Show
  • Weeks 21–22: 9 and 10 km.
  • Eat a light meal 2–3 hours before (toast, banana, oats). Drink water during runs over about an hour.
4

Easier, then the peak

▶ Show
  • Week 23: 8 km. Week 24: 11 km, your longest run.
  • Then the taper starts (tutorial 13).

5 · Common mistakes and fixes

✗ Running it too fastThe long run turns into a race.
✓ FixTalk-test pace. It should feel easy for the first two thirds.
✗ Jumping in distanceFrom 6 km straight to 10 km.
✓ FixAbout +1 km a week, with the easier weeks.
✗ Trying something newNew shoes or a new breakfast on the longest run.
✓ FixTest new things on short runs first.
✗ No recovery afterHard session the next day.
✓ FixThe day after a long run: rest or a very easy 20 minutes.

6 · Practice drill: this week

  • Plan this week's long run distance from the ladder.
  • Eat a light meal 2–3 hours before; note what you ate.
  • Run it at talk-test pace; carry water if it's over an hour.
  • Next day: rest or easy walk.
