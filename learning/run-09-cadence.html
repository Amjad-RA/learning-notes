🦶 Cadence and strides

Track: RunningBeginnerPractice: 5 min after 2 easy runs a week

1 · The core concept

Cadence is how many steps you take per minute. Taking slightly quicker, shorter steps (a small increase of about 5%) brings your foot closer to under your hips and lowers the load on your knees and hips with each step. Strides are short, relaxed speed-ups that teach your legs to move quickly without strain.

🔢Count, don't guess
Steps in 30 seconds × 2 = steps per minute.
➕Small change: +5%
From 150, try about 158. Not a big jump.
💨Strides
4–6 smooth speed-ups of about 20 seconds after an easy run.

3 · Before you start

  • Tutorial 1 done (form).
  • You can run 30+ minutes easily.
  • A flat, safe stretch of about 100 m, like a track straight or a quiet path.

4 · Step-by-step

1

Count your cadence

▶ Show
  • On an easy run, count every step (both feet) for 30 seconds and double it.
  • Write it down. Many beginners are somewhere around 150–165. There's no single "right" number.
2

Add about 5%

▶ Show
  • Multiply by 1.05: 150 → about 158.
  • Take slightly shorter, quicker steps at the same easy effort. A metronome app can help.
  • Practise for a few minutes at a time, not the whole run.
3

Strides: smooth speed-ups

▶ Show
  • After an easy run: speed up gradually over about 20 seconds (80–100 m) to a quick but relaxed pace.
  • Quick feet, tall body, loose hands. Not a sprint: effort about 7 out of 10.
4

Walk back, repeat 4–6 times

▶ Show
  • Walk back slowly to fully recover (about a minute).
  • Then the next stride. 4 to start, building to 6.

5 · Common mistakes and fixes

✗ Sprinting the stridesFlat out, tense face and shoulders.
✓ FixQuick but relaxed: about 7 out of 10. Smooth, not straining.
✗ A big cadence jumpFrom 150 to 180 overnight.
✓ Fix+5% at a time; add more only after a few weeks.
✗ Shorter steps but a faster paceThe effort goes up without you noticing.
✓ FixSame easy effort. The steps change, the effort doesn't.
✗ Strides on tired legsAfter a long run or a bad night.
✓ FixDo them after an easy run, when you feel fresh.

6 · Practice drill: this week

  • Count your cadence on one easy run and write it down.
  • Two runs: 3 × 2 minutes at about +5% cadence.
  • After 2 easy runs: 4 strides with a walk back.
  • Note how quick, light steps felt (out of 10).
